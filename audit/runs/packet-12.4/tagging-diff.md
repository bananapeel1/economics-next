# Three-pass tagging — the Economics model-answer bank

Packet 12.4, E023/E024. Generated by `node audit/runs/packet-12.4/write-tagging-diff.mjs`; do not
edit by hand. 46 Economics questions across 22 IAL sections, of which 45 arrived untagged.

**Pass 1** — the packet author, reading each question's TEXT ONLY against that topic's rows of
`audit/raw/spec-items.json`. Recorded in `pass1-tags.json` before any pass-2 or pass-3 output was
opened.

**Pass 2** — four separate agents, one per IAL unit. Each was given only its topics' input files
(`pass2-input/<topic>.json`: `questions[{key, question}]` and `leaves[{id, subtopic, requirement,
wording}]`) and instructed to read nothing else in the repository — not the marks, not the mark
scheme, not the model answer, not another pass. This is the pass packet 12.1 could not run: it had
no agent-spawn tool and fell back to the lexical matcher, and its own escalation asked for this to
be fixed before the method scaled past one section.

**Pass 3** — `audit/scripts/tag-lexical.mjs`, the deterministic lexical matcher from packet 12.1,
rule unchanged and untuned, run from the same input files.

**Only tags at least two of the three passes proposed are written.** A tag one pass made alone is
not a tag; it is a line on the review list below.

**Pass 3 is a weak third instrument on this bank.** It confirms 44 tags and abstains on most of the
rest: 69 of the 120 written tags rest on passes 1 and 2 with pass 3 dissenting, and only
7 written tags carry no pass-1 vote. The independence that is actually load-bearing here is
pass 1 against pass 2. That is a real property of a lexical matcher against a bank whose questions
are written in a teacher's words rather than the specification's, and it is the same limitation
packet 12.1 recorded; naming it is better than quoting "three instruments agreed" as if all three
had voted.

Single-pass proposals, not written: pass 1 alone 17, pass 2 alone 38, pass 3 alone 46.

## What pass 3 abstaining actually means — a fourth reader, blind, with controls

The counts above cannot distinguish two readings of the 69: pass 3 is weak on this bank, or the
two model passes share a blind spot and both proposed the same plausible-but-wrong leaf. Those look
identical from an agreement table, and the second is the failure mode `audit/DECISIONS.md` keeps
recording. So a fourth reader adjudicated a sample of `(question, requirement)` pairs one at a
time, shuffled and unlabelled, with controls it could not see either (`build-adjudication.mjs`,
seed 20260922, verdicts in `adjudication-verdicts.json`, answer key in `adjudication-key.json`).

| group | pairs | judged "examines" |
|---|---|---|
| **treatment** — written tags carried by passes 1+2 with pass 3 dissenting | 10 | **9** |
| positive control — unanimous written tags | 7 | 7 |
| negative control — leaves of the same topic that no pass proposed | 7 | 0 |

The controls are what make the treatment row mean anything: an adjudicator that said yes to
everything would have scored 7/7 on the negative control too, and it scored 0. On that evidence the
69 are mostly real tags a lexical matcher could not see, not a shared hallucination, and the
caveat above is a footnote rather than a finding.

The one rejection is recorded rather than acted on: `econ-j-curve-depreciation-8` →
`ECON-4.3.3-2e` ("The distinction between devaluation and depreciation of a currency"). The
question uses the word "depreciation" but does not ask for the distinction, and the adjudicator is
right that this is the weaker of that item's two tags. It stays, because the merge rule was fixed
before any output was read and dropping a tag on a fourth opinion after the fact is exactly the
tuning this method exists to prevent. A later packet may remove it on this evidence.

## What the disagreements say

The direction of disagreement is the same one packet 12.1 recorded, and it is worth restating with
a second instrument in the room. Pass 3 is lexical: it cannot tag a question that examines a leaf in
other words, so a question whose wording avoids the specification's own vocabulary comes back empty
from it. Pass 2 is the opposite — it reads for sense and proposes more (it proposed the most tags of
the three), including whole requirement sets where a question asks for "two factors" without naming
which two. Pass 1 declined those on principle: a question that could be answered from any two of
five leaves does not examine a particular one.

That disagreement is where the merge rule earns its keep. `barriers-development-4` is the clearest
case: pass 1 proposed nothing, and five tags were written anyway because passes 2 and 3 — which
share no code, no prompt and no author — agreed on them. `globalisation-definition-4` is the mirror
image: pass 2 proposed five, nobody else proposed any, and it stays untagged.

An untagged question is visible to `npm run spec-coverage` and reads on the page as a floor. A
wrongly tagged one is invisible, and would inflate the coverage number permanently.
