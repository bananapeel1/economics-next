packet-13.2: the drills reach students, and five of them were printing their own answers

Packet 13.1 built the quant engine and four templates and mounted none of them. 13.2 mounts them:
`lib/quant-pool.js` derives a section's drills from its spec number, Learn Mode renders one at the
check-in of the chapter that teaches it, and the Quiz tab renders every drill the section has.
Nothing is stored and nothing is authored, so no content row changed and no migration was needed.

Two defect classes had to be closed on the way, and both were inherited.

FIRST, three of the four shipped templates pointed at sections that do not exist. `ped` carried
specCode 1.2.4 and `multiplier` 2.4.2 — UK GCE numbers; every IAL section number has 3 as its
middle digit. `breakeven` carried 2.3.1, a real Business heading and the wrong one: break-even is
2.3.2 Financial planning. The pool joins template to section BY spec number, so all three would
have been mounted and rendered nowhere, and the packet would have looked finished.

  ped        1.2.4 -> 1.3.2   econ_spec.txt:576, :604
  multiplier 2.4.2 -> 2.3.4   econ_spec.txt:1056, :1079
  breakeven  2.3.1 -> 2.3.2   bus_spec.txt:885, :903
  arr        3.3.3            already right

A template now names a PHRASE as well as a number, and the test asserts the specification uses it
under that heading. The first version checked only the shape of the code and passed breakeven at
2.3.1, because that heading exists. A number that exists is not evidence that the topic lives there.

SECOND, and this is the one worth reading: FIVE OF THE EIGHT TEMPLATES PRINTED THEIR OWN ANSWER.
The variable cost in breakeven's stem could BE the contribution (1 draw in 23). arr's total net
return could be one of the three cash flows (1 in 21). payback's cash-still-to-repay could be one
of them (1 in 12). A 25% price rise could land on a new price of $25 (1 in 160). A wrong CHOICE
could be the right answer (1 in 20,000, found by a verifier counting exhaustively). Every one of
them marked a student correct for typing a number they could see, and none of quant-check's six
checks could see it: from inside a template every figure is correct, and it is the combination of
a correct stem and a correct answer that leaks. Check 7 reads the rendered card instead.

The probe that found four of the five did not look at the data. Enumerating the draw's own
parameters reuses the filter it is meant to test; leak-census.mjs builds the item and reads its
strings. printed-answer-ab.txt removes each template's rejection in turn: 229, 758, 254, 417 and 2
failures at 5,000 draws, clean with all five in place.

  4 templates -> 8 registrations across the six areas DRILLS.md names
  payback (WBS13), percentage-change x2 (WBS11, WEC12), index-numbers (WEC12)
  7 of 43 sections carry a drill        quant.unit baseline 20 -> 15 keys
  npm run quant-check   clean at 200, 2,000 and 5,000 draws

The variety rule needed fixing before any of that could be measured: it asked for 80% of the DRAWS
to be distinct, and sampling D times from V number sets can only reach V(1-(1-1/V)^D), so at 2,000
draws it failed three templates whose arithmetic is right. It is now 80% of what sampling can
reach, which makes `variants` load-bearing in both directions.

The drill is deliberately outside the quiz score. QuizTab posts {score, total} and compares a
student's best like with like on `total`; folding a generated item in would move every future
attempt from 25 to 26 and orphan every row already in the table.

FIVE verification rounds, and every one found something. Round 2 rejected D028: it asserted two
things, a fix round traded one away, and a half-true id reads as confirmed — split into D029 and
D030. Round 3 found the choice that was the answer. Round 4 defeated the guard written for it, on
a template this packet had just fixed: payback printed its two-mark months answer in two of three
choices, on every draw, because the small-integer exemption was written for a coincidence in a
stem and a choice is never a coincidence. Round 5 found the fix for THAT had left a one-mark
version of itself — the step above the year choice was labelled "Months into year 4" — so the year
now comes first and no later label names it, which makes it a better item as well as a safer one.

The rule that took five rounds to state: a figure can be individually correct and still be the
answer, and a number in a choice is never a coincidence.
